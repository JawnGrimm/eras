# Era I Milestone Work Brief — The Eanna Provisioning Undertaking

**Status:** Detailed design brief for Game Director review; portfolio direction approved, Work content and mechanics remain proposed  
**Date:** 2026-11-23  
**Authority:** `docs/GDD.md` remains canonical. This brief does not revise the GDD or lock implementation.  
**Portfolio decision:** Owner-approved Option 1 — one required milestone Work, followed by one required career-scale Work selected from two alternatives. The Ordered List remains a candidate; the second career-scale candidate remains open between The Long Delivery and The Work Group Account.

## 1. Purpose and design mandate

The Eanna Provisioning Undertaking is the shared required milestone Work for Era I. It should demonstrate that the central gameplay is administrative judgment under human and institutional constraints: people need different things, information arrives unevenly, resources move through several hands, and decisions about timing, custody, allocation, and shortfall have consequences.

It must **not** feel like Storehouse Reckoning with a larger inventory, more rows, or a longer discrepancy audit.

### Owner-approved portfolio constraints

- The Eanna Provisioning Undertaking is the required milestone Work.
- After it, the player completes one required career-scale Work selected from the authored alternatives.
- The Ordered List remains one candidate.
- The second career-scale candidate remains deliberately unresolved between The Long Delivery and The Work Group Account until the Eanna brief has developed enough to assess portfolio contrast.
- The unselected career-scale alternative is not a declined obligation and carries no penalty.
- Works remain the mandatory historical-progression layer and sole source of Historical Progress XP.
- The Storehouse Reckoning remains the Introductory Tutorial outside all three activity layers.

### Design question this Work should answer

Can the player coordinate a consequential undertaking across overlapping people and institutional responsibilities, making defensible decisions when needs, timing, resources, and evidence do not line up neatly?

## 2. GDD alignment check

The brief preserves these locked boundaries:

- Administrative judgment and consequential documents are the gameplay; tone remains grounded, human, and wry.
- Works are distinct from optional Assignments/Commissions and Challenges.
- The Storehouse Reckoning remains a tutorial, not a Work template.
- The Work uses the shared six-AP Workday and existing overtime rule. No real-time timer or four time blocks.
- Historical Progress XP remains independent of Career XP, Professional Rank, Appointments, Money, and Provisions.
- Events, Records, and Knowledge remain distinct. Evidence is not hidden or rewritten to create difficulty; character interpretation remains situated.
- Authority derives from actual appointments and responsibilities, not rank, status, or relationship alone.
- Deterministic authored rules only; no runtime generative AI for content, validation, or outcomes.
- Character death/departure does not restart the era. Play continues as an existing connected scribal NPC without inheriting the former character's skills or rank.

No numerical reward, threshold, AP cost, duration, exact calendar date, or final outcome formula is set here.

## 3. Historical frame and confidence discipline

### Strongest usable basis

Era I research supports using Eanna as the principal setting for an authored undertaking involving administrative records, commodity movement, storage/distribution, production-related activity, labor allocations, sealings, and large-scale ceremonial or public gathering contexts. Archaic accounts include categories associated with fields, yields, herds, cereals, bread/beer, and labor.

This evidence does **not** establish one fully reconstructed administrative department, a modern procurement office, a universal measure, a fixed annual festival calendar, or a complete supply chain with every origin and recipient known.

### Authored reconstruction

The following are scenario design choices, not claims about a documented historical event:

- This particular gathering and its date.
- The exact institutional purpose and scale.
- The named sponsor, counterparties, affected people, and reviewers.
- The exact goods, services, quantities, routes, production dependencies, and allocation priorities.
- The sequence of stages and the specific record set.
- The precise authority and custody arrangements used in this scenario.

The final scenario needs a claim ledger distinguishing **attested**, **inferred**, **authored reconstruction**, and **unsupported/excluded**. Historical research must verify each concrete claim before content lock.

### Guardrails

- Do not imply Eanna is a neat temple-versus-state bureaucracy.
- Do not assume every producer, workshop, field, herd, carrier, or work group is directly controlled by Eanna.
- Do not invent a standing military bureaucracy or make the Martial orientation a combat path.
- Do not treat seals as modern personal signatures. Use them as contextual evidence of closure, custody, identity, or authority where supported by the scenario.
- Do not imply every record reveals origin, final recipient, individual responsibility, or cause of discrepancy.
- Preserve commodity-specific numerical/metrological systems; do not collapse them into a universal unit.

## 4. Human situation and central tension

A substantial institutional gathering is approaching. Its needs are not met by one storehouse or one clerk. The undertaking depends on overlapping relationships and flows: resources already available, goods expected from elsewhere, production or processing, transport, custody, and allocation among people or activities.

The institution needs a defensible plan and a record of what actually happened. The player cannot simply make every need fit the available supply. They must determine which requirements are firm, which are estimates or preferences, what can arrive in time, who can authorize changes, who bears a shortfall, and what remains uncertain.

The human tension should come from **competing legitimate needs and imperfect coordination**, not from a contrived villain or a puzzle with one hidden correct answer.

Examples of competing pressures for scenario authors to test—not a fixed canonical list:

- A high-priority ceremonial or institutional requirement versus a practical need of the people doing the work.
- A timely but less certain incoming supply versus a smaller, better-evidenced local allocation.
- Protecting a promised recipient versus revising an allocation before the gathering.
- Preserving custody and traceability versus moving goods quickly enough to meet the date.
- Meeting the stated scale of the gathering versus negotiating a defensible reduction.
- Recording an unresolved shortage honestly versus attributing blame without sufficient evidence.

The player should be able to make a responsible decision that still leaves someone disappointed or a need unmet.

## 5. How this differs from Storehouse Reckoning

| Storehouse Reckoning (tutorial) | Eanna Provisioning Undertaking (milestone Work) |
|---|---|
| Teaches basic professional interaction through a guided stock/accounting exercise | Tests coordination across an undertaking involving multiple people, responsibilities, and flows |
| Focuses on acquiring/examining evidence, entering quantities, submitting, and receiving assessment | Focuses on establishing needs, planning, coordinating, making trade-offs, recording execution, and preserving a defensible outcome |
| Bounded tutorial problem with a guided solution path | Multi-stage undertaking with several viable approaches and consequences |
| The central question is whether the player can account for a storehouse discrepancy | The central question is how an institution can meet competing needs with constrained resources, uneven timing, and incomplete information |
| No Historical Progress XP; outside the three activity layers | Required Work; sole-layer source of Historical Progress XP on accepted completion |
| Not a model for future Works | Must establish a broader Work pattern without becoming a generic template for every future Work |

**Acceptance test:** if the scenario can be reduced to “inspect stock, find the missing quantity, submit the correct number,” it has failed this brief. A discrepancy may occur inside the undertaking, but it cannot be its organizing purpose.

## 6. People, responsibilities, and authority

Author only the people required to make the undertaking legible. Candidate functional roles:

1. **Sponsor / requesting authority** — establishes the institutional purpose, scope, and constraints.
2. **Resource holders / producers** — control or report particular available resources or production, without assuming direct institutional subordination.
3. **Transport / custody counterparties** — carry, dispatch, receive, guard, or account for movement where the scenario supports those roles.
4. **Affected recipients / work group** — people or activities whose needs are served or constrained by allocation decisions.
5. **Reviewer / authorizer** — assesses the account or authorizes a change within a specific appointment or responsibility.
6. **Verifier / later user** — can confirm execution or use the resulting record.

A person may hold more than one role. Every role must have an explicit basis in the scenario. Do not infer authority from seniority, career orientation, familiarity, relationship, or participation alone.

The four starting professional orientations (Civic, Religious, Commercial, Martial) should shape the protagonist's initial context, contacts, responsibilities, and plausible approaches. They should not create four separate Works or make any orientation the sole eligible route. Martial involvement, if present, remains limited to historically restrained security, custody, protection, or organized movement contexts.

## 7. Work structure: candidate stages

These stages are a design scaffold, not a locked action list. Each stage must present a decision or useful judgment, not just a form to fill.

### Stage 1 — Establish the undertaking

**Purpose:** Understand what is being organized, why it matters, who has authority, who is affected, and when the gathering is expected.

Potential player work:
- Examine the sponsor's instruction and available context.
- Separate required needs from estimates, preferences, and unsupported assumptions.
- Identify who can authorize scope or allocation changes.
- Record what is not yet known.

**Decision pressure:** accept the stated scope, seek clarification, or qualify the plan before committing resources.

**Persistent effects to consider:** accepted instruction or scope record; institutional matter linked to the Work; explicit commitments only where a person actually agrees.

### Stage 2 — Establish the resource picture

**Purpose:** Build a qualified picture of what may be available and when.

Potential player work:
- Examine relevant store, field, herd, production, and incoming-delivery information where authored.
- Distinguish recorded quantity from physical observation, expected quantity, and unverified report.
- Identify dependencies and missing evidence.
- Ask appropriate people for clarification or verification.

**Decision pressure:** rely on a plausible estimate, spend time obtaining stronger evidence, or plan around uncertainty.

**Persistent effects to consider:** observations and Records with provenance; Knowledge only for people who actually receive or examine the information; a request/commitment if a counterparty accepts one.

### Stage 3 — Negotiate the plan and allocation

**Purpose:** Make a workable plan across competing needs.

Potential player work:
- Compare supply routes and timing.
- Prioritize, reduce, substitute, defer, or renegotiate requirements where authority permits.
- Make the human consequences of shortfall visible.
- Preserve the distinction between a proposed allocation and an authorized one.

**Decision pressure:** who receives what, when, and on whose authority? What is the cost of protecting one need at the expense of another?

**Persistent effects to consider:** authorized allocation or revised scope; commitments from counterparties; unresolved requests; institutional matter updates.

### Stage 4 — Coordinate movement, production, and custody

**Purpose:** Ensure that planned flows can actually occur and that responsibility is legible.

Potential player work:
- Coordinate dispatch, production completion, transport, receipt, and storage as relevant.
- Compare seals, tags, accounts, and physical observations without treating any one source as infallible.
- Respond to delay, damaged goods, changed availability, or refusal.
- Renegotiate when the original plan is no longer viable.

**Decision pressure:** wait for a better-supported delivery, use an alternate route, change allocation, or accept a partial result.

**Persistent effects to consider:** dispatch/receipt/custody Events and Records; commitments fulfilled, revised, or breached; relevant matter state changes.

### Stage 5 — Reconcile execution and exceptions

**Purpose:** Establish what happened, what can be supported, and what remains unresolved.

Potential player work:
- Compare plan, dispatch, receipt, production, and allocation evidence.
- Distinguish delay from diversion, missing record from missing goods, and discrepancy from proof of wrongdoing.
- Attribute claims to their sources.
- Preserve uncertainty where the evidence does not establish cause or responsibility.

**Decision pressure:** close with a qualified account, seek further verification, or escalate a genuinely unresolved issue.

**Persistent effects to consider:** reconciliation records, disputed claims, follow-up matters, and evidence-based Knowledge for relevant recipients.

### Stage 6 — Present the outcome and preserve the record

**Purpose:** Give the appropriate authority a defensible account of the undertaking.

Potential player work:
- Produce a connected record set that explains the requirements, major flows, decisions, receipts, allocations, and unresolved issues.
- Present it to a person whose appointment grants the relevant authority.
- Respond to assessment or request for clarification.
- Preserve the final disposition and any authorized follow-up.

**Decision pressure:** claim completion, present a responsible partial result, or leave a defined matter open for follow-up.

**Persistent effects to consider:** assessment Event, final institutional Record, matter disposition, Knowledge transfer through plausible channels, later opportunities or obligations.

The final submission must not retroactively create all the events that supposedly occurred during the Work. Stage effects are committed when the player takes the consequential action.

## 8. Approaches and outcome space

At least three broad approaches should be viable, with no single approach universally optimal:

1. **Fulfil the original scope:** prioritize the best-supported supply plan and attempt to meet the stated requirements.
2. **Renegotiate the undertaking:** seek authorized changes to quantity, timing, scope, or priority before the shortfall becomes unavoidable.
3. **Deliver a qualified partial result:** protect the most defensible priorities, document what cannot be met, and preserve unresolved issues for follow-up.

Within those approaches, player choices may favor speed, stronger verification, reduced risk, or protection of particular recipients. The scenario must expose meaningful trade-offs rather than hide a single answer in skill checks.

Outcome dimensions to author and assess:
- Fulfilment of declared requirements.
- Timeliness relative to the authored gathering window.
- Evidence quality and provenance.
- Custody and receipt traceability.
- Whether allocation changes were authorized.
- Treatment of affected people and competing needs.
- Honest preservation of uncertainty.
- Quality and usability of the final record.

Do not collapse these dimensions into a single moral score. A timely but poorly supported outcome may differ from a late but defensible one. A responsible partial outcome can be accepted as a real historical undertaking.

## 9. Deliverables

The Work should produce a coherent record set, not a modern standardized document suite. Candidate functional deliverables:

- An initial statement of purpose, scope, authority, and timing.
- Resource observations and qualified availability records.
- One or more allocation/plan records, including authorized revisions.
- Dispatch, production, receipt, or custody records where the scenario uses those flows.
- A reconciliation or closing account that preserves units, provenance, and uncertainty.
- A final institutional disposition record and any follow-up matter.

The actual record types, formats, and material presentation must be grounded in Era I content research and the existing content standards. Avoid modern labels such as requisition form, purchase order, universal ledger, or standardized chain-of-custody certificate unless clearly identified as internal design terminology rather than in-world artifacts.

## 10. Work lifecycle edge A — succession during an active Work

This is a required design problem because stage actions can write persistent Events, Records, commitments, and matter updates before final submission. The undertaking cannot simply disappear or be reset when the player character dies or leaves play.

### Recommended continuity principle

**The institutional undertaking and its committed history persist; control of the undertaking does not automatically transfer with the player character.**

Separate three things in the design:

1. **Work definition and institutional undertaking:** the authored Work, its originating matter(s), committed stage outcomes, durable records, unresolved conditions, and institutional commitments.
2. **Character-specific activity:** the former character's own observations, interpretation, personal obligations, and actions, all preserved with their provenance.
3. **Current player control:** the newly active, already-existing connected scribal NPC, with their own skills, rank, familiarity, relationships, knowledge, and history.

### Proposed succession handling

When death/departure occurs mid-Work:

1. Preserve every committed Event, Record, Knowledge grant, matter update, fulfilled or unfulfilled commitment, and Work-stage result. Do not roll them back or replay them.
2. Preserve the former character's contribution and provenance. Do not rewrite their work as the successor's work.
3. Re-evaluate the undertaking from the successor's actual position: their knowledge, capabilities, relationships, location, authority, and access.
4. Continue only through a plausible institutional or interpersonal handoff. The successor must be invited, assigned, sponsored, or otherwise given a credible route to take responsibility.
5. Do not transfer the predecessor's skills, rank, familiarity, private Knowledge, personal authority, or personal commitments to the successor.
6. The successor may examine existing records if they have access and a plausible opportunity. Their own interpretation may differ; objective evidence remains unchanged.
7. If the successor cannot reasonably continue, the institution may pause, narrow, reassign, or close the undertaking through an authored deterministic response. Do not fabricate a replacement person or force an implausible handoff.
8. Unfulfilled personal commitments remain attached to the person who made them. They do not silently become the successor's obligations. An institution or counterparty may renegotiate, reassign, or record the resulting gap.
9. A successor who formally accepts responsibility creates their own commitments from that point forward. Their acceptance is a new Event, not inherited state.

### Work-state recommendation

Represent the undertaking's durable progress independently from the active character's identity. A Work should have stable links to its institution, originating matter(s), committed stage outcomes, records, and current responsible person/role. Character-specific observations and actions retain actor provenance.

This is a design requirement, not a proposed exact schema. Engineering should inspect the existing Work save contract before recommending the smallest compatible representation. Avoid a broad new simulation subsystem.

### Failure and recovery cases to test

- Death immediately after an irreversible stage effect.
- Departure after a commitment is accepted but before it is fulfilled.
- Successor has access to the records but lacks the predecessor's familiarity or skill.
- Successor is connected to the profession but lacks the appointment required for authorization.
- No plausible connected successor can take over the undertaking.
- Institution reassigns or narrows the Work; previous records remain valid and attributable.
- A successor declines the handoff; no automatic personal misconduct penalty absent a real accepted obligation.

### Open implementation/design questions

- Does the active Work remain in the player's journal as an institutional undertaking after succession, or does it move to an institutional matters view until a handoff occurs?
- Which authored authority can offer the handoff, and what minimum connection/access/capability is required?
- What happens to any Work-specific UI state when the player character changes?
- Can the Work be formally closed as incomplete, and how is that distinguished from abandoning a personal obligation?

The brief recommends continuity of the institutional undertaking and an explicit handoff, but exact eligibility rules and data representation remain open.

## 11. Work lifecycle edge B — date window and single-active-Work concurrency

The current engine supports one active Work at a time and has no general suspend/resume action. That is an implementation fact, not an approved permanent design rule. The milestone brief must avoid invitation timing that makes this limit feel arbitrary or traps the player between two required undertakings.

### Recommended first-playable policy: clean calendar sequencing

For the initial Era I portfolio:

1. The Eanna Provisioning Undertaking is the first required Work invitation and the first required Work the player is expected to undertake.
2. The career-scale Work invitation is not issued into an overlapping active window. Its authored invitation should arise after the milestone reaches a defined disposition (accepted completion or an authored, recoverable closure state).
3. The calendar and invitation text explain why the second undertaking becomes available when it does.
4. The milestone's date-anchored gathering window remains visible, but the player is not forced to spend all AP on it or finish in one Workday.
5. The milestone must include a catch-up route if the original gathering date/window is missed. Catch-up should change the circumstance or scope in a historically plausible authored way; it must not silently pretend the original date never passed.
6. No Assignment-style three-day offer expiry applies to the Work invitation.
7. The player should not be presented with two required Works whose active windows overlap while the engine prevents switching between them.

This sequence fits the approved progression shape—milestone first, one selected career-scale Work second—and allows the first implementation to retain one active Work without presenting that technical limit as a universal design law.

### Later alternative: explicit suspend/resume

If playtesting demonstrates that clean sequencing makes the world feel too linear or blocks meaningful opportunities, investigate suspend/resume as a later extension. It would need:
- Explicit pause and resume actions or equivalent clear state transitions.
- A visible account of what continues in the world while a Work is suspended.
- Deterministic consequences for commitments, deadlines, and changing circumstances.
- Clear rules for which stages remain available after resumption.
- Save/restore and succession behavior for multiple persisted Work states.
- UI that makes active, suspended, at-risk, and recoverable undertakings distinguishable.

Do not implement suspend/resume merely to future-proof the system before playtesting establishes the need.

### Required concurrency QA

- The milestone invitation appears while no other required Work is active.
- The player can progress over multiple Workdays without spending all six AP on the Work.
- The career-scale invitation is not issued while the milestone remains active.
- The player misses the original date and receives a clear catch-up route.
- A completed or formally closed milestone enables the next required career-scale invitation.
- A succession handoff does not create a second active Work or duplicate the invitation.
- Assignments and Challenges remain independently available under their own rules and do not resolve the Work automatically.

## 12. Invitation and catch-up details

The invitation must communicate:
- Why this undertaking exists now.
- Who is requesting it and what authority they hold.
- Why this protagonist is being approached.
- The broad scope and people affected.
- The known gathering date/window and what is expected to change if it passes.
- What is known, estimated, or uncertain.
- Whether accepting creates any explicit personal commitment.

Declining or delaying is not automatically misconduct. The consequences of waiting must arise from the actual authored circumstance. If the gathering passes, a catch-up route might be a retrospective account, a reduced follow-up allocation, or a successor institutional need—but the selected route must be authored and historically qualified. It is not an infinitely repeatable copy of the original gathering.

## 13. Work engine and world-simulation requirements

The existing engine already supports typed Work definitions, evidence packets, AP-costed actions, prerequisites, scenario progress, deterministic assessment, recovery, and finalization. The current production catalog contains the tutorial, not this milestone. Current persistent world effects are concentrated at finalization; a general stage-to-world-effect result is not yet established.

The brief therefore requires design/engineering collaboration on:

- A typed, deterministic stage effect contract for Events, Records, Knowledge grants, commitments, matter updates, and notices.
- Stable links between the Work and its originating institutional matter(s), people, institution, and relevant records.
- Provenance for each committed stage effect and actor.
- Idempotent application: saving/restoring or re-evaluating a stage must not duplicate effects.
- Clear distinction between proposed plan, authorized plan, and executed/verified action.
- Succession-safe separation of institutional Work progress and character-specific actions.
- Deterministic invitation and catch-up selection.
- Authority checks against actual appointments/responsibilities.
- Multi-day persistence and save/restore at each stage.
- No dependency on optional Assignments or Challenges for a viable required path.

Do not prescribe a new schema until Engineering has compared these requirements with the existing state and transition contracts. No schema change is approved by this brief.
