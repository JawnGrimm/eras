# Assignment Orientation and Institutional Context Specification

**Status:** APPROVED — BEHAVIORAL CONTRACT  
**Date:** 23 November 2026  
**Authority:** Subordinate to `docs/GDD.md`; implements the approved professional-orientation principle.  
**Scope:** Assignment authoring, contextual access, and the relationship between professional orientation and institutional circumstances.  
**Implementation:** None. This specification does not authorize runtime changes.

## 1. Approved design principle

**Professional orientation influences both the Assignments a character encounters and the circumstances under which they perform them. Shared administrative responsibilities create common ground; institutional specialization creates meaningful differences.**

The four orientations—Civic, Religious, Commercial, and Martial—are professional emphases, not classes, exclusive institutions, or separate Assignment queues. The world has one shared pool of authored opportunities. A character encounters opportunities through their actual place in the world: institutional position, relationships, current work, knowledge, access, and circumstances.

The same broad administrative craft can appear in different institutional settings without becoming the same Assignment with a cosmetic label. Purpose, people, evidence, procedures, authority, and consequences should fit the actual situation.

## 2. Three authoring distinctions

Use these as content-authoring labels, not mandatory runtime types.

- **Shared:** A broadly recurring administrative responsibility that can plausibly arise across multiple institutional settings. Examples include accounting for goods, recording personnel or dependents, reconciling deliveries, tracking property, or preparing a report.
- **Contextual:** A shared responsibility whose purpose and playable circumstances are materially shaped by a particular institution, event, or relationship. The institution changes what is being protected, who relies on the record, which evidence matters, what procedure is appropriate, and who may act on the result.
- **Specialized:** A responsibility tied to a particular institutional function or setting, with access beyond that setting possible only where a plausible route is authored.

An Assignment may combine these qualities. The labels do not imply a fixed taxonomy or a hierarchy of importance.

## 3. Keep contextual variation lightweight

Do not build a four-orientation variant engine inside each Assignment.

Prefer, in this order:

1. **Institution-anchored Assignment definitions.** Author separate, normal Assignment definitions where the underlying craft is shared but the actual task differs. For example, a storehouse tally and an overland dispatch check may both require quantity reconciliation, but they have different sources, custody chains, people, risks, and recipients.
2. **Lightweight framing or contact hooks.** Where the playable task genuinely remains the same, use a small amount of authored context to explain why this character receives it, who approaches them, or what institutional perspective matters.
3. **A single multi-context definition only when justified.** Use branching within one definition only where the shared evidence, action model, and outcome contract are genuinely stable and the branches are few and bounded.

Do not author four copies merely to mirror the four orientations. Equally, do not force unlike institutional work into one deeply branching template to avoid separate definitions. Avoid multiplying authoring, validation, and test burden under another name.

## 4. How orientation changes play

Orientation may affect both:

### 4.1 Encounter and access

- Which authored opportunities are plausible in the character's current professional world.
- Which people or institutions have a reason to approach the character.
- Which delivery channel or contact introduces the opportunity.
- Whether a cross-institutional request, appointment, relationship, or circumstance provides a credible route.

### 4.2 Circumstances and performance

- The purpose and stakes of the task.
- The institution's procedures and expectations.
- The people involved and their interests.
- The records, material evidence, and information available.
- The character's familiarity with the relevant administrative context.
- The authority the character has—or does not have—to inspect, alter, authorize, release, or execute.
- The consequences for people, obligations, property, and institutional decisions.

Orientation is not a substitute for any of these underlying facts. It may shape the character's likely network and exposure, but must not silently grant competence, authority, knowledge, or a relationship.

## 5. Eligibility, qualification, and authority are distinct

Keep these questions separate:

1. **Access:** Is there a credible route by which this opportunity reaches this character?
2. **Qualification:** Does the character have the skills and other supported capabilities needed to perform the task?
3. **Authority:** What actions is the character permitted to take in this situation?
4. **Interpretation:** What does the character recognize or understand from the evidence, given actual skills, familiarity, experience, and circumstances?

Familiarity is narrow, provenance-backed exposure. It is not competence or qualification. Skills determine ability. Appointments or other explicit grants determine formal authority. Relationships may explain contact, trust, or willingness only where those facts are supported. Do not infer access, trust, or authority from a single aggregate relationship score.

A character may receive a task they are not fully qualified to perform if the authored situation supports supervised work, a bounded contribution, or an opportunity to learn. The Assignment must make the limits and consequences legible; it must not silently waive requirements.

## 6. Shared work does not mean identical work

A common administrative subject—goods, people, property, transport, or accounts—does not make two Assignments interchangeable.

For every contextual or specialized Assignment, author the details that materially shape play:

- **Purpose:** Why does this institution need the work now?
- **People:** Who requests, supplies evidence, receives the document, assesses it, and acts?
- **Evidence:** Which records and physical observations exist, who produced them, and what do they establish?
- **Procedure:** What comparison, inspection, consultation, or document is appropriate here?
- **Authority:** What may the player do, recommend, or not do?
- **Consequence:** What decision or obligation may follow, and who owns it?
- **Access route:** Why did this character receive the opportunity?

Do not add all fields mechanically when they have no bearing on play. The aim is a coherent human situation, not paperwork for its own sake.

## 7. Cross-institutional access

Orientations are not hard walls. A character may encounter work outside their usual setting through an authored, plausible route such as:

- a personal or professional relationship;
- a specific request or referral;
- an appointment or delegated responsibility;
- shared custody, delivery, transport, or other ongoing work;
- an event that brings institutions or people into contact.

The route must be visible in the opportunity's context or delivery explanation. Do not fabricate generic professional reach merely to make an Assignment available. Do not require every orientation to receive every Assignment.

## 8. Historical and content constraints

Historical plausibility governs the institution and procedure, not just the names and props.

- Distinguish attested evidence, cautious inference, and authored reconstruction.
- Do not present a fictional scenario or workflow as a documented historical event.
- Do not import modern departments, standardized offices, job titles, procedures, or military structures without period-specific support.
- The four orientations should be expressed through the institutions and relationships supportable in the current era.
- For Era I Uruk, Civic and Religious overlap substantially; Commercial concerns exchange, procurement, delivery, and networks without assuming later private merchant houses; Martial remains a restrained orientation expressed through specific security, movement, mobilization, or accountability matters—not an invented standing army or military scribal corps.
- The Era I institutional responsibility matrix and historical audit constrain the literal settings and content. A modern temple/regimental analogy is a design test, not historical evidence.

## 9. World-generation and progression boundaries

- One shared Assignment opportunity pool; no four separate queues.
- Authored definitions own the task, context, evidence, procedure, and outcomes. The generator discovers and presents eligible authored opportunities; it does not invent scenario content.
- A world matter or NPC need does not automatically become an Assignment. The authored opportunity definition must establish that it is playable professional work.
- Access rules must use supported state and explicit authored rules. Do not infer eligibility from orientation alone where no institutional or personal route exists.
- Assignments remain optional. They do not automatically generate or unlock Challenges and are not prerequisites for Works.
- Assignments and Challenges do not award Historical Progress XP. Works remain its sole source.
- No runtime generative AI for Assignment generation, record validation, or outcome determination.
- A quiet board or no eligible offer is valid; do not create work to fill an orientation quota.

## 10. Acceptance criteria for authored content

An Assignment set satisfies this contract when:

1. Each definition has a clear shared, contextual, and/or specialized rationale.
2. Its institution, purpose, people, evidence, procedure, authority, and consequences cohere.
3. The access route explains why this character receives it.
4. Orientation influences both plausible encounters and the circumstances of work, without becoming an exclusive class gate.
5. Familiarity, skill, access, knowledge, and authority remain distinct.
6. Cross-institutional access is possible where a credible route is authored, not assumed.
7. Contextual variation is implemented through separate institution-anchored definitions or lightweight hooks by default—not an unbounded variant matrix.
8. The content is historically dated and its evidence/reconstruction status is explicit.
9. It remains optional and independent of Challenge/Work progression.
10. Its absence from a character's board is explainable by actual circumstances, not arbitrary quota or hidden orientation filtering.

## 11. Production handoff boundary

Production's first step is a cross-system compatibility audit of all consumers of Assignment availability, authored context, and delivery channels before proposing technical changes. Specifically inspect the relationship delivery channel used by South-Court Delivery Reconciliation (Enlil-kisu) against the approved Era I character-history contract (`era1-character-history-v1` / GD-CHARACTER-START-001): EN-01 through EN-04 record an existing NPC as a factual `PersonLifeEvent` participant, but deliberately create no numeric trust, affection, or other `PersonRelationship` dimensions. Determine exactly what a `relationship` delivery channel currently means and inspects. If eligibility requires an existing relationship dimension/score, it may silently exclude a character whose only supported connection is a factual LifeEvent participant. Test that case explicitly; do not solve it by fabricating relationship scores. Decide whether the channel is merely explanatory/provenance metadata or an eligibility predicate, and specify the supported factual-connection route if access is intended.

The live implementation baseline inspected for this approval has an important distinction: `validChannels()` in `src/engine/opportunities.ts` currently validates a `relationship` channel by checking that the referenced Person exists; it does not inspect a relationship score. `generateDailyAssignments()` in `src/engine/assignmentGenerator.ts` currently discovers matching active institutional matters and presents authored definitions without filtering on delivery-channel relationship dimensions. This does not remove the audit requirement: Production must trace all consumers and preserve the intended semantics when access rules are expanded.

The first implementation step is a compatibility proposal: identify what current definitions and generator already support, what is missing, and the smallest deterministic extension needed. This document does not prescribe TypeScript types, schema fields, module placement, save migrations, or transition API signatures. Those belong to Production after the behavioral contract is approved.

## 12. GDD alignment and department impact

**GDD alignment:** Implements the four professional orientations and the GDD's shared institutional world, situated knowledge, optional Assignment layer, and evidence-based professional judgment. It does not alter activity layers, AP, progression, rank, authority, or the historical-progression source.

**Cross-department impact**
- **Game Design:** Author shared/contextual/specialized opportunities and credible access routes.
- **Historical Research:** Verify institutional setting, period, terminology, evidence, and reconstructed procedures.
- **Engineering:** No material implementation decision until the contract is approved; then audit current generator and eligibility consumers.
- **Art/UI:** Present institution, people, location, and material evidence clearly; no orientation-specific queue presentation.
- **Audio:** No material impact.
- **Production/QA:** After approval, test distinct set-and-setting across orientations, including cross-institutional access and the absence of arbitrary filters.
