# Era I Assignment Portfolio — Historical Research Brief

**Status:** RESEARCH REQUEST — DESIGN INPUT, NOT APPROVED SCENARIO CONTENT  
**Commissioning workstream:** GD-OPPORTUNITY-001, Track 1  
**Period:** Uruk, c. 3200 BCE; Late Uruk / Uruk IV–III transition  
**Owners:** Historical Research, coordinating with Game Design  
**Purpose:** Establish defensible institutional settings and parameter domains for an Era I Assignment portfolio of roughly 8–9 distinct anchor definitions plus recurring, deterministically parameterized institutional occurrences.

## 1. Research objective

The current three recurring Assignment activities all sit in the Temple Storehouse under Ur-Nanna. They form a useful initial cluster but do not establish four meaningfully distinct professional settings.

Research should help Game Design:
- close the Commercial and historically restrained Martial coverage gaps;
- distinguish Civic and Religious responsibilities without imposing a modern state/church division;
- identify credible institutional contexts, people, evidence, authority, and consequences for approximately two anchor opportunities per orientation's setting;
- identify repeated administrative needs and valid parameter ranges for recurring occurrences, so the shared Assignment pool can sustain the soft ~8 AP/day supply expectation without static repetition or fabricated busywork.

This brief requests evidence and bounded scenario seeds. It does not approve any named scenario, office, procedure, title, or runtime mechanic.

Use these project documents as starting points, not as substitutes for primary or scholarly evidence:

The existing orientation audit finds:

Its classifications and examples remain hypotheses for research, not established facts.

## 3. Priority research questions

### A. Commercial — highest priority

Investigate plausible Late Uruk exchange, procurement, transport, and custody relationships that could support a genuinely distinct Assignment setting.

Questions:
1. What material evidence supports movement of raw materials and finished goods into, through, and out of Uruk at this horizon?
2. Which commodities, origins, routes, counterparties, and institutional relationships can be discussed with confidence? Which are inferred or reconstructed?
3. What evidence exists for measuring, accounting, sealing, receiving, dispatching, or documenting transfers between distinct parties?
4. What can be said about the role of institutional agents, carriers, intermediaries, craft producers, and exchange partners without assuming later private merchant houses or a standardized merchant profession?
5. Which human-scale disputes or administrative uncertainties are plausible—quantity, quality, custody, seal integrity, delayed delivery, substitution, or responsibility—and what evidence would actually establish them?

Deliverable emphasis: at least two distinct Commercial anchor concepts with different counterparties, institutional purposes, evidence, and consequences. A re-skinned Storehouse receipt check is insufficient.

### B. Martial — historically restrained, high caution

Investigate specific security, mobilization, organized movement, logistics, custody, and accountability contexts. The objective is not to prove a formal military bureaucracy.

Questions:
1. What direct evidence exists for organized armed activity, conflict, security, protection, defensive labor, or movement of people and goods around c. 3200 BCE?
2. What can be inferred about the organization of collective movement, transport, provisions, equipment/material custody, or accountability—and at what confidence?
3. What evidence supports named actors, decision rights, orders, or authority chains in a specific scenario?
4. Which contexts could plausibly involve a scribe or recordkeeper without inventing a military scribal corps?
5. Which tempting reconstructions should be rejected or explicitly labeled speculative?

Deliverable emphasis: at least two candidate contexts only if the evidence supports them. Prefer narrow, defensible situations over a broad fictional military institution.

Do not present any of the following as established Uruk IV practice without new, direct evidence: standing army, standardized conscription, ward quotas, muster rolls, military scribe corps, quartermaster office, fixed regiment, universal military ration table, or a standard professional army hierarchy. Do not use the later Uruk city wall as proof of a c. 3200 BCE military system; date any defensive structure independently.

### C. Civic and Religious — distinguish responsibilities within overlap

Research the practical responsibilities that could make Civic and Religious play feel different while acknowledging that institutional, economic, political, and cultic activity overlap.

**Civic questions**
- What evidence supports administration of land, labor, production, storage, redistribution, collective work, maintenance, and public/monumental activity?
- Which responsibilities and records are attributable to an institution, work group, or authority, rather than to a modern civil service?
- Which tasks could produce meaningful accountability, allocation, or public-work decisions?

**Religious questions**
- What evidence supports offerings, ritual goods, ceremonial provisioning, sacred property, cultic maintenance, or the people responsible for cultic activity?
- What can be said about the relationship between cultic activity and institutional storage, labor, production, and distribution?
- Which responsibilities can be distinguished by purpose, recipients, timing, evidence, and authority without inventing a separate church bureaucracy?

Deliverable emphasis: identify distinct responsibilities and stakes, not merely different labels for the same Temple Storehouse inventory task. Civic and Religious concepts may share institutions, personnel, and resources where historically justified.

### D. Recurring institutional occurrences and parameter domains

Identify recurring or repeated administrative needs that could plausibly yield distinct Assignment occurrences over time.

Investigate:
- recurring allocations, deliveries, receipts, procurement, production, storage, inspection, maintenance, labor organization, or record preparation;
- attested commodities and the measurement systems appropriate to each;
- plausible counterparties and institutional relationships;
- settings and custody transitions;
- evidence patterns that could support a real discrepancy or uncertainty.

For each proposed parameter family, state which combinations are supported, which are merely plausible reconstructions, and which are invalid. Do not mix units across commodities or assume one universal measure. Do not manufacture discrepancies; show the source records or circumstances that would make a discrepancy a defensible authored event.

## 4. Required deliverables

### 4.1 Evidence and confidence register

For every material claim, provide:
- claim and its relevance to a playable administrative situation;
- evidence class: textual, archaeological, iconographic, architectural, comparative, or other;
- source citation, including publication/page, tablet/object/catalogue number, excavation context, or stable corpus reference where available;
- chronology and phase attribution;
- confidence: attested, inferred, reconstruction, or unsupported/reject;
- interpretive disagreement, dating uncertainty, and limitations.

Separate what the source directly shows from scholarly interpretation and from the proposed game reconstruction.

### 4.2 Anchor scenario seeds

Provide a shortlist sufficient to support approximately two credible anchor opportunities per orientation's institutional context. For each candidate, identify:
- institutional setting and why it fits the orientation context;
- actors/counterparties and what each plausibly knows or controls;
- purpose and immediate human/operational problem;
- available records, material evidence, and gaps;
- plausible player contribution and procedural boundary;
- who holds decision/authorization authority;
- possible consequence for people, goods, obligations, or institutional decisions;
- access route by which a junior scribe could plausibly receive the work;
- evidence confidence and explicit reconstruction elements.

These are scenario seeds, not finished Assignment scripts or claims that a documented event occurred.

### 4.3 Recurrence and parameter matrix

Provide a compact matrix of supported parameter values and compatible combinations for:
- counterparty;
- commodity and appropriate unit system;
- source/receiving quantities or other discrepancy type;
- location/setting and custody transition;
- institutional purpose and recipient;
- relevant record, sealing, observation, or testimony.

Identify combinations that must not be generated together. State which values can vary between occurrences without changing the underlying activity contract and which variations would require a distinct bespoke Assignment.

### 4.4 Negative-claim list

List common but unsupported or anachronistic assumptions that the Era I authoring team must avoid, with source-based explanations.

## 5. Historical constraints

- The opening date c. 3200 BCE sits around the Late Uruk / Uruk IV–III transition. Chronological schemes vary; avoid false precision.
- Uruk's archaeological record is uneven. The better-documented contexts do not establish every office, room function, procedure, or chain of authority.
- Eanna is a major and exceptionally important administrative/cultic precinct, but do not reduce Uruk to one fully unified temple bureaucracy.
- Do not assume a simple division of Eanna as civic/economic and Anu/Kullaba as a separate ritual administration. Verify any building, dedication, or institutional claim for the exact phase.
- Do not treat modern categories—state, church, private merchant, department, regiment, professional army—as self-evident equivalents.
- Attestation of an object or sign does not automatically establish its function, reading, or the identity of the person using it.
- Keep historical uncertainty visible in internal authoring notes and player-facing claims where material.

## 6. Research method and source expectations

Prioritize:
1. Primary archaeological/textual corpora, excavation publications, museum catalogues, and object-level records.
2. Peer-reviewed scholarship and specialist monographs on Late Uruk administration, economy, exchange, institutions, and conflict.
3. Reputable reference databases and institutional resources for corroboration and cross-checking.

Use stable citations. For proto-cuneiform evidence, identify the corpus/tablet or catalogue reference when possible; do not cite a broad summary as though it proves a specific workflow. For iconography, separate depicted subject from its interpretation. For comparative evidence from later periods or other sites, label it comparative and do not silently project it backward into Uruk IV.

## 7. Handoff to Game Design

Historical Research should return:
- the evidence register;
- ranked anchor scenario seeds;
- the recurrence/parameter matrix;
- the negative-claim list;
- explicit questions that cannot be answered from available evidence.

Game Design will then choose which concepts to author, distinguish evidence from reconstruction, and specify playable actions, deliverables, choices, and consequences. Production is not asked to implement this research brief.

**Priority order:** Commercial and Martial gaps first; Civic/Religious differentiation alongside them; recurrence/parameter evidence in parallel.
